I watched Brandon Sanderson's talk about why he is opposed to AI in art and how he opposes it because we are the art. I think he had some really good points about AI and art from the creator's perspective and did a good job of tackling some of the important points related to art. However, as someone who primarily consumes art rather than produces art, I don't know if he touched on the consumer side of art enough. One challenge with art is that it can be used for different purposes, and as in the example he gave with John Henry, I think we will see that certain types of art will simply become preferred as AI-generated content. What's tricky is that some art fulfills a more utilitarian purpose while other art is intended to capture and convey meaning and connection. 

I also researched image content accountability, and I thought that some of the current initiatives are really interesting. I think that incorporating some kind of metadata or invisible watermarking is a good idea, though I'm curious how effective it could be and how well it would dissuade people with bad motives from using AI-produced images in poor ways. Theoretically, if the biggest models incorporated features like this, then AI use could be controlled a lot better than it is now. Regardless of legislation, I think it's important to be transparent about when we are using AI and how we are using it. AI can make many things easier, but it is important to verify what it produces and take accountability for any products that are created part or in whole with AI.
