I watched/listened to a few different videos and podcasts talking about agentic misalignment, including discussion on the results from the Anthropic study. I also spent a while browsing discussion forums such as Reddit to see what others thought about these developments, and there was a pretty big range of opinions on the topic. One idea that I found useful was that AI models know the language of ethics well, but that is not necessarily the same as adhering to ethical principles. Ultimately, AI models are mathematical in nature, and models are trying to optimize to meet certain goals, and they don't care or genuinely recognize if the methods for accomplishing that are unethical in nature. I found other discussion about the dangers of anthropomorphizing AI, which reminded me of counsel from church leaders about the same issue—these models don't have feelings, even if the way they act sometimes make it seem as though they have human characteristics, reasoning ability, and feelings. While some people saw the Anthropic experiments from a gloomy perspective (particularly news outlets for which it is useful to have good clickbait headlines), I found that many people did not see this as a particularly large concern, noting that the scenario is pretty contrived and that it's hard to see see that they can or will necessarily translate to real behavior. One study I found attempted to replicate the Anthropic experiments but did not find the same level of bad behavior from AI agents. 

For me, one of the biggest takeaways I have surrounding what decisions AI should and should not be able to make is that there will always be a need for some degree of human intervention. I don't believe that AI models will ever be able to do things that they aren't explicitly granted permission to do. There are some real safety concerns, but I think that it is possible to give AI the capability to do great things while also making sure that humans are able to oversee AI output and decision making, especially for important decisions that have real-world consequences. We should use AI to improve productivity, but ultimately we should ensure that humans retain the reigns and that we are the ones with the ultimate agentic authority. I personally don't love the idea of giving AI such extensive access to the email system as in the Anthropic study, but there are plenty of tools that I would be comfortable giving AI access to provided that there is some human oversight.
