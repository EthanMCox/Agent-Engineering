## Things as They Really Are 2.0
I think that Elder Bednar's thoughts have some overlapping applications with my regular life and with my work as an agent engineer, as well as some differences. It's certainly useful and important to recognize that chat bots and similar programs are no more than mathematical algorithms and don't actually reflect intelligent thoughts or feelings. That is good to know from a user perspective, but I think it's also helpful to view engineering in that way as well. Although I don't see a problem with tailoring chat bots to have specific roles or personalities, I also think that the engineer should strive to make it clear to the user that the bot is not *really* intelligent in a human sense. 

I thought that Elder Bednar's discussion about artificial intelligence and agency was also really interesting considering that we use "agents" in "agent engineering." As an engineer, I hope that the agents I create allow other people to better exercise their own agency rather than to be acted upon. As Elder Bednar said, these tools can be used to accomplish good things, and I think that it is wise to be thoughtful about both *what* I make in addition to *how* I make it. Although I can't necessarily prevent people from using my tools for bad purposes, I can seek to design tools with the intention of genuine helpfulness and goodness.

## Appropriate Use of AI Chatbots
The discussion from class was really interesting to me. Some students mentioned feeling uncomfortable with Claude's Constitution, but I actually thought it was really cool, and I like the value-based system that Claude is being trained with. Of course, I don't think that AI chatbots should be used as if they are real people, but I actually don't mind the idea of these tools being encoded with some sense of personality or a value system. For example, one of Claude's primary focuses is to be helpful, which I don't view as inherently bad. On the other hand, using AI for the purposes of pseudo-romantic conversations rubs me the wrong way. 

As with all things, AI can be used for good and bad purposes. I don't have any issues with chatbots being given particular roles or special training so that it embodies specific attributes, but I do think that they should be used in ways that, like Elder Bednar discussed, encourage the use of moral agency. For example, I attended a church lesson the other week in which the content was almost certainly all AI generated, and I felt that it was absolutely an inappropriate use of AI.
